What the Results Suggest

In this dataset, price-to-book ratio had the strongest positive association with 3-year returns — companies with higher P/B ratios (typically growth or quality firms) tended to have better subsequent returns in the 2021–2024 period. Net margin also showed a positive link, consistent with the idea that higher profitability is rewarded over time.

Counterintuitively, FCF yield and operating margin show negative coefficients. This may reflect the period: 2021–2022 was a market environment where high-growth companies with compressed current margins (Amazon, etc.) outperformed high-yield value names. It may also reflect P/E already capturing some of this information, causing collinearity.

Debt-to-equity had the weakest association — suggesting leverage alone was not a strong predictor of returns across this sample.

The model explains only 11.1% of return variance (adjusted R² 7.1%), which is both a limitation and an interesting finding in itself: it aligns with the efficient market hypothesis that most price movements cannot be explained by publicly available fundamentals.

Methodology

Annual fundamentals were pulled via yfinance for 98 large-cap U.S. equities across fiscal years 2021–2022. Features with over 50% missing values were dropped; remaining missing values were median-imputed. All features were winsorized at the 2.5/97.5th percentiles to reduce distortion from extreme ratios, then standardized using z-scores.

A multiple linear regression (sklearn LinearRegression) was fitted using standardized metrics as features and the forward 3-year total price return as the target. Fiscal year 2022 observations use Dec 2022 → Dec 2025 as the return window; 2021 observations use Dec 2021 → Dec 2024.

Limitations

  • Survivorship bias: only companies currently trading are included. Failed, delisted, or acquired firms are excluded, which likely overstates average returns.
  • Correlation ≠ causation: a high coefficient does not imply the metric drives returns — both may be driven by a third factor.
  • P/E and P/B values reflect current yfinance data, not true point-in-time historical values at each year-end.
  • Small sample relative to features: 164 observations and 7 predictors increases overfitting risk. No out-of-sample validation was performed.
  • Short time horizon: only two fiscal years (2021–2022) were available via yfinance, limiting generalizability. A paid data source (Compustat, FactSet) would enable a longer panel.
  • Returns are raw and not risk- or market-adjusted.
